Clock Settings Cards

A friendly getting-started guide

Nothing stalls a peaceful evening like a digital chess clock beeping in menu mode. These cards do two things: keep the honest facts of the common time controls at hand, and — the real trick — let you write down your own clock's steps, one card per preset, so the next game starts from memory instead of a manual hunt.

How to use it

  1. Two words to know: increment adds time after each move (clock grows back); delay waits a few seconds before charging you (clock pauses, never grows). Both are kindnesses to slow writers.
  2. How settings are written: “G/90 +30” means game in 90 minutes, plus 30 seconds added per move. “G/3 +2” gets fast and sweaty fast.
  3. Grab one card per time control you actually play. Most club players live on three or four; nobody needs nine cards.
  4. Set it on your clock once, slowly, writing each button press or menu step on the card. That opus of scribbles is the free manual your clock never came with.
  5. Tick “used it in a game” with the date. Three ticks on a card is a sign that preset is truly yours.
Pencils only. Clock menus get revised by firmware updates and memory lapses alike — pencil steps can be corrected without ceremony.
Beginner tip: Most clocks remember the last preset used. If games start slow because you keep re-inventing the evening, pick one or two presets to be your house defaults and leave the rest of the menu alone.
