Lesson Notes

A friendly getting-started guide

Lessons leak. Whatever a coach said, a book showed, or a “one quick idea” video claimed — if it is not written down within the hour, half of it quietly escapes. This page catches it: the idea, the example line, the homework, and the question you did not get to ask.

How to use it

  1. Header goes in first. Topic, date, and where it came from (coach, book, course, or that video you keep re-watching).
  2. Key ideas: no more than three. If the lesson had seven, your notes should hold the two that will change your next game — that filtering is practice, not cheating.
  3. Example lines: write the moves in the numbered slots, in pairs like the scoresheet. Even a rough line beats a perfect memory of it.
  4. Homework rows: copy the drills you were assigned, and put a date by each. Undated homework at every level becomes decorative.
  5. Questions for next time: the wise habit. Leave two lines — you will have some by the time you reach the car.
Pencils only. Chess study should change its mind as you deepen — a pencil lets an unhelpful note be corrected instead of becoming permanent bad advice in your own handwriting.
Beginner tip: Do not write the whole lesson down. Write the one position you want to show a friend — if you can't yet explain it in one pencil line, that's the real gap the next study session should target.
